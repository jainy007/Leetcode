# AEB + FCW closed-loop SIL: report

AEB is the second feature built on the `can_function` framework, after ACC. It adds four
things: a feature module (`tools/gen/features/aeb.py`), a logic template
(`templates/features/aeb/`), a plant observer (`sil/plant/.../aeb_observer.*`) and a step
catalogue (`sil/steps/features/aeb.yaml`). The framework itself needed no changes: receive, E2E
checks, timeouts, bindings and send are generated by the same code as for ACC.

## Inputs
- **Requirements:** 14 EARS requirements (`examples/aeb/requirements.yaml`) in the spirit of
  UN R152 and Euro NCAP AEB Car-to-Car. The values are project choices, not normative.
- **Interface:** the same vehicle bus as ACC, plus `AebCommand` (state, warning, deceleration).
- **Cascade:** warning at TTC ≤ 2.8 s. Partial braking (4 m/s²) at TTC ≤ 1.8 s, but only after
  at least 0.8 s of warning. Full braking (8 m/s²) at TTC ≤ 1.2 s. Kickdown (≥ 80 % accelerator)
  cancels; the brake pedal does not.

The calibration was chosen with a design prototype, and the performance requirements claim only
what the design meets with margin:
- a stationary target is avoided at 20–60 km/h;
- impact speed at 80 km/h is at most 40 km/h;
- a target driving at 20 km/h is avoided from up to 80 km/h.

## Results
| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Requirements | 14 / 14 PASSED in the trace matrix |
| Generated SIL tests | 32, all passing (plus generated codec and component tests) |
| Mutation testing (`python3 -m tools.mutate aeb`) | 27 of 29 detected, 2 declared equivalent, 0 unexpected; every calibration value has a mutant |

## How the tests were strengthened
The first mutation run found 8 mutants that survived without being equivalent. Each one showed
a gap in the scenarios:

| Surviving mutant | Why the tests missed it | Added |
|---|---|---|
| full-brake TTC 1.2 → 1.0 s | only "8 m/s² reached eventually" was checked | full-braking onset TTC in [1.1, 1.3] s |
| minimum warning time 0.8 → 0.6 s, or ignored | with a steady approach, the TTC thresholds already space warning and braking by 1 s | lead vehicle braking hard, so only the 0.8 s rule keeps them apart |
| partial deceleration 4 → 3 m/s², or full value used for partial | no check on the first braking stage | "emergency braking started with 4 m/s²" |
| warning release 3.3 → 2.9 s | the warning ended because closing stopped, not because of TTC | lead accelerating at 2 m/s²: the warning ends by TTC (3.3 s) while still closing at 2.2 m/s |
| kickdown ignored in FULL_BRAKE | kickdown was only tested during partial braking | kickdown during full braking after a cut-in |
| driver brake cancels AEB | AEB re-triggered within two cycles, so the end result hid the cancellation | the braking stage must persist 40 ms after the brake press, for both PARTIAL and FULL |

Calibration mutants added later (all detected): max target range 150 → 100 m, min closing speed
0.5 → 2.5 m/s, standstill speed 1 → 5 km/h. Structural coverage (MC/DC) then led to scenarios for:
- the end of a warning by lane loss and by kickdown;
- the 150 m relevance limit.

## Equivalent mutants (with reasons)
- **Closing-speed threshold dropped:** a closing speed below 0.5 m/s never reaches TTC 2.8 s
  within the 150 m range.
- **150 m range check dropped:** TTC ≤ 2.8 s at more than 150 m needs a closing speed above
  53 m/s, which is beyond the 150 km/h speed limit. The check is defensive only.

## Measurement tolerance
TTC is measured by the plant from its own world state, not from the radar. Expected TTC windows
use a tolerance of 0.1 s, made up of the 40 ms radar period, the 20 ms cycle and the 0.1 m
range quantization.

## Limits
These are the same plant limits as for ACC: point-mass vehicles, first-order actuator lag, ideal
radar apart from quantization, one target. There are no pedestrian or crossing scenarios, since
the radar interface carries only one longitudinal object.
